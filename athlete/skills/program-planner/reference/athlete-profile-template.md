# Athlete Profile: Essential Coach Context — <Athlete name>

> Lives at `athlete/<slug>/personal-profile.md` and is **gitignored** — it holds medical detail
> and is never committed. See `athlete/README.md`.
>
> These are the **durable** facts: true across blocks, revised when something actually changes,
> never re-asked at the start of every block. Anything specific to one block belongs in that
> block's Program Planning Doc instead. Every section here is a question `program-planner` will
> otherwise have to ask, so a blank section costs a round of questions; write "unknown" rather
> than deleting the heading, so a gap stays visible.
>
> Keep it short. This is read in full before every plan, and a long profile is a profile whose
> stale lines nobody notices.

## Overview
Age, bodyweight, archetype (CrossFit / rugby / combat / general hybrid / pure strength), training
age and years of structured training, current weekly training pattern, and the long-horizon goal
the individual blocks serve.

## Strength Baseline
Key lifts with a number, a date and how it was established (tested, estimated from a top set,
self-reported). Technical proficiency with barbell, gymnastics and Olympic lifts — a proficiency
ceiling changes exercise selection as much as a load does.

## Primary Constraint
The one thing that shapes programming most: a tendinopathy, a joint, a medical condition, a
schedule. State its current status, the **pain-monitoring model** it is managed under (thresholds
during, after, and next morning), and any medical clearance or red flag. `program-builder` and
`review-workout-log` both apply the rules written here, so write them as rules, not as history.

## Aggravators and Movements to Dose Carefully
What reliably provokes the constraint above, what to avoid outright, and what is fine in a
specific dose or position. Name the movement and the reason — a bare "no lunges" gets substituted
badly by anyone who does not know why. Rename this section after the tissue if that reads better
for this athlete.

## Preferred Structure
Training days per week and which days, session time cap, and training environment (home /
commercial gym / box) with any shared-equipment or footprint etiquette limits. Then the equipment
reliably available: rower, ski, air/assault bike, sled and turf, rig or rack, KBs, DBs, boxes,
GHD, machines. Absence is as load-bearing as presence.

## Conditioning Direction
Energy-system emphasis, impact tolerance, and appetite for running, jumping and high-skill
metabolic work. Note anything the constraint above rules out.

## Recovery / Monitoring
Sleep, nutrition, stress, and which metrics actually exist (HRV, readiness, RHR, wearable).
Autoregulation preference. **Any history of overreaching, illness under load, or training
through a warning sign** — that history is what the plan turns into explicit hard stops, so it is
the section most worth being specific in.

## Movement Preferences
Disliked and enjoyed movements, and appetite for novelty versus familiarity. Adherence is a real
programming variable; a well-designed block that goes untrained is worth nothing.

## Coach Summary
Three or four sentences a coach could read alone and prescribe safely from. What matters most,
what to protect, and what this athlete's failure mode looks like.
